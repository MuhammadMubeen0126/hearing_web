Myth vs Fact

Common Hearing Myths, Explained

Understanding hearing health starts with separating common assumptions from practical facts.

✓

Hearing loss only affects older adults.

Myth

Hearing loss can affect only seniors.

Fact

People of many ages can experience hearing difficulties from noise exposure, illness, injury or other causes.

✓

If I can hear, my hearing is fine.

Myth

Being able to hear sounds means hearing is normal.

Fact

Difficulty understanding speech, especially in noise, can be an early sign of hearing difficulty.

✓

Hearing loss is just a minor inconvenience.

Myth

It is something you can simply ignore.

Fact

Untreated hearing difficulty can affect communication, social participation and quality of life.

✓

Turning the TV up solves the problem.

Myth

Louder sound is always the answer.

Fact

When speech clarity is reduced, simply increasing volume may not make conversations easier to understand.

✓

You should wait until hearing gets much worse.

Myth

There is no benefit to checking hearing early.

Fact

A hearing assessment can establish a baseline and help identify changes before they become more disruptive.

✓

Hearing aids make everything sound unnatural.

Myth

Hearing aids are one-size-fits-all and sound the same for everyone.

Fact

Professional fitting and ongoing adjustments are important for comfort, clarity and natural sound.

Understanding the Difference

Why Hearing Myths Can Be Misleading

Hearing changes do not always happen in an obvious way. Some people notice that sounds seem quieter, while others mainly notice that conversations are harder to understand. This is one reason hearing concerns can be easy to dismiss at first.

A hearing assessment provides more useful information than simply asking whether someone can hear. It can help identify patterns in hearing ability and give a clearer starting point for discussing communication needs.

A useful rule of thumb: if hearing difficulty is repeatedly affecting conversations, the television volume, phone calls, meetings or social activities, it is reasonable to discuss those changes with a hearing professional.
More Questions

Hearing Myth & Fact FAQ

Can a hearing test be useful even if I am not sure I have hearing loss?

Yes. A hearing assessment can provide a baseline and help clarify whether the difficulties you notice are consistent with a hearing change.

Why do I hear people but struggle to understand them?

Hearing involves more than detecting sound. Speech understanding can become more difficult when there is background noise or when certain sounds are harder to hear clearly.

Should family members encourage someone to get their hearing checked?

Family members often notice communication changes first. A supportive conversation focused on everyday experiences can be more helpful than criticism or pressure.

Does everyone with hearing difficulty need hearing aids?

Not necessarily. The appropriate next step depends on the person's hearing results, communication needs, preferences and professional recommendations.
