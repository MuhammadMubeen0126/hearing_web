The Assessment

It starts with listening to you.

Hearing difficulties can be gradual. You may notice that people mumble, the television needs to be louder, or restaurants become exhausting long before you describe yourself as having hearing loss.

A professional assessment puts those experiences into context. We discuss your hearing concerns, communication goals and the environments that matter most to you before explaining the testing process.

01

Your Hearing History

We talk about changes you have noticed, previous hearing care and situations where communication feels difficult.

02

Listening Measurements

Appropriate hearing measurements help establish a clearer picture of your hearing ability.

03

A Conversation About Results

Your results are explained in practical language so you understand what they mean for everyday life.

After Testing

What do the results actually mean?

The value of an assessment is not just the test itself. It is having someone explain the findings and help you understand your options.

Understanding Your Hearing

We discuss the pattern and degree of hearing difficulty identified during your assessment and how it may relate to your communication experiences.

Deciding What Comes Next

Depending on your needs, next steps may include monitoring, further assessment, communication strategies or a discussion about hearing technology.

No Pressure

An assessment is an opportunity to get information. You can take time to consider what you have learned and what feels right for you.

Questions

Before Your Appointment

How long does testing take?

Appointment length depends on the type of assessment and your individual needs. The clinic can provide an estimated appointment time when you book.

Do I need to wait until hearing is very poor?

No. If communication is becoming harder, testing can give you useful information before difficulties become more disruptive.

Will testing mean I need hearing aids?

No. Testing provides information. Recommendations depend on your results, communication needs and preferences.

What should I bring?

Bring relevant hearing history information and think about the situations where you have the most difficulty hearing.
